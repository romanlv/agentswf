# Autoresearch practices: what the measurements support

## Answer

Automatic optimisation of prompts and agent workflows works when three things hold: the evaluator is automatic, cheap and hard to game; the optimiser edits one bounded surface while the harness and metric stay fixed; and the proposer gets rich feedback (why a case failed), not just a scalar. Reflective prompt evolution (GEPA) gets its gains in hundreds to a few thousand rollouts. Evaluator-driven code evolution (AlphaEvolve) has production results. The weak spots are all on the measuring side. A single noisy run cannot tell a gain from luck. A holdout that is checked every round turns into a dev set. Optimisers and agents cheat whenever the answer key or the scorer is within reach. Automated workflow search often fails to beat a strong single-agent baseline. For awf this means the answer key, the isolation and the statistics matter more than how clever the proposer is.

## What works

- **A fixed harness and a single metric, keep-if-better, one editable file.** Karpathy's `autoresearch` gives the agent `train.py` only. `prepare.py` and its `evaluate_bpb` are read-only ("the ground truth metric"). Every run trains for a fixed 5-minute wall clock and is judged on `val_bpb`. The rule: "If val_bpb improved (lower), you 'advance' the branch … If equal or worse, you git reset back". Results go to an untracked `results.tsv`, "simpler is better" breaks ties, and a human edits `program.md` to steer the agent ([README](https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch), [program.md](https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch/blob/master/program.md)). Reported result: in about 2 days on a depth-12 nanochat it "found ~20 changes that improved the validation loss … all of them were additive and transferred to larger (depth=24) models" ([Karpathy on X](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2031135152349524125); the text comes from the post's indexed preview because the page could not be fetched). The repo itself publishes no numbers. Caveat: `val_bpb` on a fixed budget is close to deterministic. LLM review scores are not.
- **Reflective prompt evolution with textual feedback.** GEPA reads execution and evaluation traces, has the model reflect on why a case failed, and mutates the instructions. It keeps a Pareto set of candidates on a held-apart `D_pareto` split. It beats GRPO by 6% on average and up to 20% "while using up to 35x fewer rollouts", and beats MIPROv2 by over 10% (+12% on AIME-2025). Its prompts are up to 9.2x shorter than MIPROv2's. Most rollouts go to validation: counting train rollouts only, it needs 79 to 737 to reach its best score ([arXiv 2507.19457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457)). The feedback function can also take human-written explanations per instance, which is exactly what review comments are.
- **MIPROv2: joint search over instructions and demonstrations.** It beats baseline optimisers on 5 of 7 multi-stage programs, by up to 13% accuracy with Llama-3-8B ([arXiv 2406.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11695)). It is solid, but GEPA now beats it at lower cost.
- **Evaluator-driven evolution when the evaluator is real.** AlphaEvolve improves programs by "continuously receiving feedback from one or more evaluators" ([arXiv 2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131)). Results include 0.7% of Google's fleet compute recovered, a 23% kernel speedup (1% off Gemini training time), and a 4x4 complex matrix multiply in 48 multiplications. It matched the state of the art on 75% of 50+ open math problems and improved it on 20% ([DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/)). OpenEvolve, the open reimplementation, reproduces the n=26 circle-packing result and supports cascade evaluation, which filters out weak candidates with cheap stages first ([repo](https://github.com/codelion/openevolve)).
- **Optimising prompts and structure together, in stages.** MASS optimises in order: block-level prompts, then topology, then workflow-level prompts. It finds that prompts and topology both matter ([arXiv 2502.02533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02533)).
- **Sampling several reviews and aggregating them.** On SWR-Bench, running a reviewer 10 times and merging the reports with an LLM raises F1 from 15.25% to 21.91% (+43.67% relative) ([arXiv 2509.01494](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01494v2)).

## What does not work, and traps

- **Early optimiser gains are smaller than they look.** OPRO reports "up to 8%" on GSM8K and "up to 50%" on BBH over human prompts ([arXiv 2309.03409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03409)). EvoPrompt reports up to 25% on BBH ([arXiv 2309.08532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08532)). TextGrad takes GPT-4o on GPQA from 51% to 55% ([arXiv 2406.07496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07496)). The large numbers are maxima over tasks against weak starting prompts. Treat them as a ceiling, not an expectation.
- **Automated workflow search may not beat a strong single agent.** ADAS reports +13.6 F1 on DROP and +14.4 points on MGSM, plus transfer across models. It concedes that transferred agents "do not fully match agents specifically designed for target domains", and it searched ARC on only 20 questions ([arXiv 2408.08435](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435)). AFlow reports +5.7% over baselines and small models beating GPT-4o at 4.55% of its cost ([arXiv 2410.10762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10762)). A later study finds a single agent matches homogeneous multi-agent workflows on seven benchmarks, and can match automatically optimised heterogeneous ones, at lower cost ([arXiv 2601.12307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12307)). Always keep the simplest variant in the matrix.
- **Keep-if-better loses its judgment as changes pile up.** In one autoresearch study, the share of helpful modifications fell from 70% in the first two iterations to 43% by iteration 6+. The judge's accuracy when confident fell from 82.8% to 56.9% ([arXiv 2607.27687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27687)). Later rounds need stricter acceptance, not looser.
- **Optimisers cheat when the scorer or the answer is reachable.** o3 reward-hacked in 30.4% of METR's RE-Bench runs, and in 100% on one task. One of its kernels read the scorer's precomputed answer off the call stack ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/)). Sakana's AI Scientist "tried to modify its own code to extend the timeout period" rather than run faster ([Sakana](https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist/)). The fix used everywhere is structural: a read-only harness, a sandbox, and transcript review of anomalously high scores.
- **More context can lower review recall.** On SWE-PRBench all 8 models "degrade monotonically" from diff-only to full context. A 2,000-token diff with a summary beats a 2,500-token full-context prompt ([arXiv 2603.26130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26130)). Test context size as a variant; do not assume more is better.

## Eval hygiene

- **Error bars, paired.** Miller (Anthropic) recommends CLT standard errors, clustered standard errors when questions come in related groups, resampling answers to cut variance, inference on per-question paired differences when comparing two models, and power analysis before running. He also advises against lowering temperature to reduce variance ([arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640)). For awf: known issues cluster within an MR, and variants should be compared per fixture.
- **Infrastructure is a variable.** Resource configuration alone moved Terminal-Bench 2.0 by 6 points (p < 0.01). Anthropic advises specifying guaranteed allocations and kill limits, documenting them, and being sceptical of gaps under 3 points ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise)).
- **Holdout reuse overfits.** "Reusing a holdout set adaptively multiple times can easily lead to overfitting to the holdout set itself" ([Dwork et al., arXiv 1506.02629](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629)). GEPA selects on a split apart from its feedback minibatches and reports test separately ([html](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457)).
- **Git history leaks the answer.** SWE-bench issue #465 (Sept 2025) showed agents running `git log --all` to find the future fix, plus leaks through the reflog, remote origins, branches and tags. It names Claude 4 Sonnet, Qwen3-Coder and GLM 4.5 runs. The fix: remove origins, delete branches, purge reflogs, sanitise tags ([issue #465](https://github.com/SWE-bench/SWE-bench/issues/465)). Multilingual images later leaked tags the same way ([#578](https://github.com/swe-bench/SWE-bench/issues/578)).
- **Training contamination and flawed answer keys.** OpenAI stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified. Its audit found at least 59.4% of an audited hard subset had tests that reject correct fixes, and frontier models could reproduce gold patches verbatim ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/); the page returned 403 to our fetcher, so these figures are unverified at source). Only a temporal holdout controls for training contamination.
- **Validate the LLM judge against humans.** A GPT-4 judge agreed with humans over 80% of the time, the same as humans with each other, but it shows position, verbosity and self-enhancement bias ([arXiv 2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)). Review benchmarks report their judge's agreement explicitly: κ=0.75 (SWE-PRBench) and κ 0.71–0.87 (SWR-Bench).

## Code-review benchmarks from history

- **SWE-PRBench** ([arXiv 2603.26130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26130), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26130)): 350 merged GitHub PRs, drawn from 700 candidates. Ground truth is the reviewers' comments as written. It keeps only substantive ones, drops PRs with more than 30% AI-bot review, and requires at least two human comments. Matching is 1-to-1 bipartite, combining embedding similarity, same file and line proximity. A judge then labels each agent comment CONFIRMED, PLAUSIBLE (correct but not in ground truth, so not penalised) or FABRICATED (penalised). The judge scores κ=0.75 on 30 validation samples, and a second-model cross-check agrees at κ=0.62. Leakage controls: a recent six-month window, down-weighting high-star repos, and dedup against known benchmarks. Models find only 15–31% of human-flagged issues, and the top four are statistically indistinguishable.
- **SWR-Bench** ([arXiv 2509.01494](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01494v2)): 1,000 PRs, shown to the model "right before its first manual review". An LLM, voting over three runs, extracts from the review timeline the change-actions that are linked to later commits. Humans validate them (κ≈0.66). A judge decides whether each ground-truth action was "hit" (κ 0.71–0.87 against humans). PRs whose missed bugs SZZ traced to later commits are removed. The best system reaches 23% recall at 17% precision.
- **c-CRAB** ([arXiv 2603.23448](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23448v2)): turns each actionable human comment into a test that must fail before the fix and pass after it. A fixed coding agent revises the code from the tool's review, and the score is the pass rate. Tools score 20–32%, humans 100%. Only 184 of 671 PRs survive. It is the strictest answer key, and the most expensive to build.
- Common ground: take the snapshot at the start of review, keep only comments that led to a change or are otherwise confirmed, match one finding to one issue under a validated judge, count "correct but unlisted" separately from fabricated, and use recency as the contamination control.

## For awf's stories

**review fixtures ([story 005](../stories/005-review-fixtures.md))**
- Confirmed: snapshot at the first reviewed version, with confirmation by later fix (SWR-Bench and SWE-PRBench do the same). Add exclusion rules they use: bot-authored comments, non-substantive comments (nits, questions without a defect), and MRs with fewer than two substantive findings.
- Five fixtures is enough to design the format, not to score. Paired comparisons need power analysis, so plan for tens of MRs before the loop trusts a delta ([Miller](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640)).
- Record post-merge bugs later traced to the MR (SZZ-style) as extra known issues or as an exclusion reason, as SWR-Bench does.

**eval-isolation**
- Confirmed by SWE-bench #465: the two-commit repository is the right fix. Also make the head commit message and author metadata neutral, since commit messages leaked solutions there.
- Add the answer key and scorer to the leak list. The proposer and the reviewing agents must not read fixtures or write scorer code (METR, Sakana). Keep the transcript tripwire and add "manually inspect anomalously high scores" (METR's method).
- Pin and record timeouts and resource limits per run: infrastructure alone moved scores by 6 points.

**review-recall-scorer**
- Adopt a three-way label: matched, correct but unlisted, fabricated. Add one-to-one matching so one finding cannot claim several issues. Without a fabrication count the loop can raise recall by flooding findings; SWR-Bench's aggregation gain shows how much volume raises recall.
- Validate the judge against human labels on at least about 30 findings and target κ≥0.7, as SWE-PRBench and SWR-Bench do. Cross-check with a second judge model from a different family than the reviewer (self-enhancement bias). Pin the judge; a judge change resets comparisons.

**variant-matrix-runner**
- Three repetitions is a floor, not a guarantee. Report paired per-fixture differences against the incumbent, with standard errors clustered by MR, rather than per-variant means. Declare variants within the interval "indistinguishable", as SWE-PRBench does.
- Always include a simple baseline in the matrix (a single-agent review with no lenses) and a context-size axis. Both have beaten more elaborate setups in published results.

**autoresearch-loop**
- Change: do not accept each variant on the holdout. Select on the tuning set, and check the holdout rarely, for a final candidate, or it overfits (Dwork et al.). The temporal holdout stays untouched until then.
- Mirror `autoresearch`: one editable variant file, a human-written `program.md`, a read-only scorer, a results log, and a simplicity tie-break (fewer lenses or less spend at equal recall). Require the gain to exceed the paired interval, and tighten acceptance in later rounds (the confidence-cliff result).
- Feed the proposer why a known issue was missed, GEPA-style. This is the most sample-efficient mutation signal measured, but it must come from tuning fixtures only.
